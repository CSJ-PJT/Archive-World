# Archive City Urban Grammar V1

Archive City is generated through `Building → Family → Block → Street → District → City`. Architecture Grammar owns a building's form and material language; Urban Grammar owns parcel, public realm, mobility, density, landscape, and skyline rules.

Every visual review must pass at Building, Family, Block, Street, District, and City scale. A visually good building fails if its block, district identity, or city rhythm fails.

## District DNA

| District | Street identity | Block / skyline rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ArchiveOS | riverfront civic plaza, premium pedestrian axis | landmark towers around public open space |
| Ledger | formal financial and administrative streets | medium/high-rise office gradient and civic squares |
| Market | narrow blocks, storefronts, food streets, night lighting | fine-grain active frontage |
| Nexus | campus loop, research courts, service utility lanes | clustered R&D and production compounds |
| Logistics | truck courts, loading lanes, container yards | large horizontal parcels near highway access |
| Residential | school walkways, parks, community streets | apartment courtyards with stepped height transition |
| Infrastructure | rail, bridge, water, port, and utility corridors | connectivity and safety override building frontage |

## Non-negotiable contracts

- A block generator places families; it never scatters individual buildings randomly.
- Repetition is capped at two visible occurrences per camera for any identical building asset.
- District DNA is evaluated from streets and public realm before facade detail.
- Generated assets remain external until a dedicated canonical approval.
- This phase creates rules and skeletons only; no city layout, runtime manifest, or canonical library is modified.
